# GRIMMWICK — Story Bible
*"Relight the Night"*

## Logline

The night before the once-a-century Ember Moon ceremony, a shadow swallows the flame that keeps Grimmwick's spirits sweet — and the only one it overlooked is the kid everyone said was too small to matter. As Pip frees the town's five corrupted guardians, each one remembers a piece of who the shadow really is... and the answer isn't a monster. It's the town's oldest mistake.

## Tone

Cute-spooky, warm-hearted, gently funny. Scary the way a haunted hayride is scary. The villain is loneliness, and the final weapon is kindness. (Think: Luigi's Mansion charm + Paddington sincerity + a Saturday-morning Halloween special.)

## The Name (canon — this is the story's deepest secret)

The town is called **GRIMMWICK**, and nobody remembers why. That is not flavor — it is the mystery's first clue, hiding on the title screen the whole time:
- **GRIMM** — the town's forgotten co-founder. He helped plant the first pumpkin, dug the first well, hung the first lantern. The founders' stone in Ravenmoor bears two names; one has been scratched out — by its own owner.
- **-WICK** — three meanings braided together: the *wick* of a candle (the part that carries the Everflame — the town that holds the light), **Granny Wick** (the seamstress whose thread and thimble stitch the story's heart), and the Old English *-wick* meaning "village" (as in Warwick, Gatwick). Read plainly, GRIMMWICK means **"Grimm's village."**

The whole town says its own name every day without hearing it. When Pip hands Grimm the invitation in the finale, the last line of the game can land it: *"Welcome home to Grimmwick. It was always your town too."*

## The World

**Grimmwick** is the town where Halloween never ends — skeletons deliver the mail, ghosts run the bakery, and the moon is always full. At the town's heart burns the **Everflame**, a giant jack-o'-lantern bonfire whose light keeps every spirit friendly and every candy sweet. Once every hundred years, the **Ember Moon** rises and the whole town gathers to recharge the flame at the **Festival of Lights** — the biggest party in the spirit world.

Tonight was supposed to be the third Festival ever held.

## Main Characters

**PIP** — the smallest kid in Grimmwick: messy chestnut hair, a pumpkin-orange hoodie, sneakers, and a candy bag. The one human face in a town of spirits — which is exactly why players anchor to Pip instantly. Gran's homemade ghost-sheet costume (two eye-holes cut slightly crooked) is Pip's most treasured possession, worn whenever Pip chooses (it's the heirloom costume in the wardrobe). Silent hero, expressive body language. Everyone calls Pip "little one." Everyone says Pip is too small for adventures. The shadow overlooked Pip for exactly that reason — and that mistake saves the town. The costume was sewn by **Granny Wick** — Pip's grandmother — for Pip's very first Halloween, before she crossed to the Quiet Side. Pip has worn it every night since.

**GRANNY WICK** — Pip's gran, a warm, round old spirit with sewing-needle hairpins and a laugh like wind chimes. She was Grimmwick's seamstress; half the town's costumes have her stitches in them. She now rests on the **Quiet Side** — where spirits go when they're done with the bustle of Grimmwick — but she is *not* done being a grandmother. Reunited with Pip in Ravenmoor Cemetery (see District 2), she gives Pip her **Silver Thimble**: squeeze it, and she'll come running. Even from the Quiet Side. *"Grans are good at that."*

**MAYOR BOO** — round, top-hatted, mustachioed ghost mayor. Kind, flustered, narrates the quest. Calls Pip "little one" the whole game... until the last scene.

**GRIMM, THE FORGOTTEN GUEST** — the shadow. Not a demon, not a ghoul: a shadow is what's left of a spirit that no one remembers. He stole the Everflame and shattered it into five embers, corrupting the guardians who found them. He wants the whole town to feel the dark he's lived in for a hundred years. *Who he really is* is the mystery the whole game unspools.

**THE FIVE GUARDIANS** — beloved protectors of the five districts, each corrupted by a stolen ember burning where their heart should be:

| Guardian | District | Before the corruption | Corrupted into |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gourdon, the Pumpkin King** | Pumpkin Patch | Jolly harvest-keeper with a booming laugh; grows the candy crops | A raging tyrant hoarding "HIS" candy |
| **Mossgrave, the Tombstone Titan** | Ravenmoor Cemetery | Gentle keeper of the ancestors' rest; can recite every name carved on his stone | A slab of fury whose bats blot out the moon |
| **Broomhilda the Broom Witch** | Witchwood | Sweet potion-maker who brews the festival cider and taught half the town to fly | A shrieking dive-bomber who set every broom in the wood flying wild |
| **Captain Wraith** | Ghost Harbor | The ferryman — brings every visiting spirit to the Festival, never missed a crossing | A cold phantom whose grief DRIED UP THE SEA — the harbor is a cracked seabed, his beloved ship beached where the water left it |
| *(final)* | Cursed Castle | — | **Grimm's Cursed Cauldron**: Grimm brews the stolen embers in a giant possessed cauldron, shadow arms rising from the black brew |

**ZOE THE WITCHLING** — Broomhilda's apprentice, found hiding in Witchwood. Comic relief, gives hints, becomes playable later.

## The Story

### Act 1 — The Night the Flame Went Out *(intro + hub)*

The town square is packed for the Festival of Lights. The Ember Moon rises. And at the exact moment of the ceremony, a shadow pours out of the castle on the hill, **swallows the Everflame whole**, and shatters it into five embers that streak across the sky into the five districts. Every grown spirit in town slumps into a gloomy sleepwalk. The guardians who rush to recover the embers are corrupted by them.

The shadow checked every threat in Grimmwick. It never checked the smallest one. Pip — too little to sit in the front row, too little to be noticed — is left wide awake in the dark square, holding a candy bag.

Mayor Boo (barely holding himself together): *"Being small has its perks, little one. Bring back the embers. Relight the night."*

### Act 2 — Five Districts, Five Truths *(the 50 levels)*

Each district is ten levels of the darkness getting worse, ending at a corrupted guardian. **And here is the engine of the story: every guardian, the moment they're freed, remembers one detail about the shadow.** The player assembles Grimm's identity like a puzzle, one boss at a time:

**District 1 — Pumpkin Patch.** The pumpkins have gone feral; the scarecrows point the wrong way on purpose. Freed, Gourdon laughs his old laugh, then goes quiet: *"The shadow... I KNOW that shadow. He helped me plant the very first pumpkin, back before the first Festival. Why did I ever forget that?"*

**District 2 — Ravenmoor Cemetery.** The bats pour from the crypts like smoke; the epitaphs have gone unreadable. Freed, Mossgrave rumbles his clue: *"There are two names on the founders' stone I guard. One belongs to the first Mayor. The other... no one ever visited it, and I cannot read it anymore — because he scratched it out himself."*

Then the big gravestone softens: *"But there is another name here that gets visited every single week. Someone left cookies on it again. You know the one, little Pip."* One grave in his garden begins to glow — and **Granny Wick** rises out of the moonlight, takes one look at Pip, and straightens the crooked eye-holes of the costume she sewed. Then leaves them crooked. *"I like them that way."*

She can't stay — the Quiet Side calls its spirits home — but she presses her **Silver Thimble** into Pip's hand: *"Squeeze this when the night gets too big for you, and I'll come running. Even from the Quiet Side. Grans are good at that. Just... give me a moment to catch my breath between visits. Crossing over is no small thing for an old ghost."* (→ **the Summon** — see mechanics. Her "catching her breath" is the in-world recharge timer.)

This scene is the story's secret weapon: the game is about what happens when a town *forgets* someone — and here, in the middle of a graveyard, it shows what it looks like when somebody is *remembered*. Pip visits. Pip leaves cookies. That's why, three districts later, Pip is the only one in Grimmwick capable of handing Grimm an invitation instead of a final blow. Pip learned it from Gran.

**District 3 — Witchwood.** Every broom in the wood flies riderless; the trees have braided themselves into mazes. Freed, Broomhilda gasps: *"He was my first flying student! He kept falling off, and everyone laughed and laughed. He never came back to class."* She gives Pip her broom — *"He never got to fly. You fly for him."* (→ the rideable broomstick.)

**District 4 — Ghost Harbor.** The sea is GONE — when the flame shattered, the water went out like a tide that never came back. Pip crosses a cracked seabed past stranded wrecks and dying tide-pools to reach the Salty Phantom, beached on its side. The lighthouse is dark; the ferry bell rings with no one pulling it. (When Wraith is freed, the sea comes rushing home — the whole district floods back to life.) Freed, Captain Wraith stares at his logbook: *"One hundred years ago tonight, I had one passenger booked for the first Festival. The fog came in thick. I never picked him up. He waited on the far shore... all night."*

By now the player knows: **Grimm was invited once.** To the *first* Festival. The guest of honor who was never picked up, laughed at when he fell, scratched off the stone, and finally — forgotten. A spirit no one remembers becomes a shadow. He didn't steal the light because he loves the dark. He stole it because he couldn't watch the party happen without him a third time.

### Act 3 — The Cursed Castle *(finale)*

The castle clock has been stopped at midnight for a hundred years. Inside the throne room: the **Cursed Cauldron**, where Grimm brews the stolen embers into endless night — shadow arms, glowing eyes, goo tides, and shadow-copies of everything Pip has fought.

The fight can't be won by hitting harder. Pip feeds the four recovered embers to the four burners around the arena; each one sweetens the brew from black to candy-pink, and the shadow shrinks — from monstrous, to man-sized, to *small*. Smaller than Pip.

And then the game asks for the one button that isn't an attack.

Pip walks up to the trembling little shadow and holds out a homemade Festival invitation — crayon letters, crooked as Pip's eye-holes: **"GRIMM. GUEST OF HONOR."** The first one ever addressed to him by name.

The cauldron cracks. The Everflame roars back. And the whole town — awake now, watching from the doorway — gets to feel appropriately terrible for exactly four seconds before Grimm bursts into tears and the Festival of Lights finally happens, a hundred years late, with its original guest of honor in the front row. Where the seats for the small ones are.

Mayor Boo, handing Pip the last ember to place: *"After you... **big hero**."*

**Post-credits:** the harbor at dawn. Something washes ashore: a lantern, frozen solid, glowing faintly blue from inside. Captain Wraith picks it up and frowns. *"...this isn't one of ours."* (→ Season 2: **Winterfest**.)

## Why this story works for THIS game

- **The mystery is the retention hook.** Five breadcrumbs, one per boss — the story reason to finish all 50 levels is wanting the next piece of who Grimm is.
- **Skills are blessings.** Each freed guardian thanks Pip with their power (Gourd Slam, Bat Swarm, the Broom, Phantom Dash, Shadow Leap) — mechanics and story are the same event.
- **The Summon is the heart.** Granny Wick's Thimble (unlocked at District 2) is Pip's panic button and the story's emotional core in one mechanic — and it is the reason Pip's final act of kindness toward Grimm feels earned instead of unexplained.
- **Granny Wick is sacred.** The Summon is never sold, never skinned, never in the pass. Some things aren't for the store.
- **The secret takedowns are story-flavored.** You beat the ghost with light, the gravestone with his own bats, the shadow with an invitation. Observation and empathy beat brute force — which is the theme.
- **The ending is the age rating.** No one dies, the villain is healed, kindness is the finisher. 4+, parent-friendly, review-quotable.
- **Seasons are canon.** Every new Festival = a new season of content: Winterfest (frozen lantern), future festivals for future districts. Grimm, now the Night Watchman, is the recurring guide — and a playable character.

## The theme in one line

*Nobody is too small to relight the night — and no one stays a monster once someone remembers their name.*
